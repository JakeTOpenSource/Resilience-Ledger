# Bridge Card

A one minute brake to run on anything you are about to accept as true.

Use it on model output, a paper paragraph, a claim in a thread, or a summary of
your own work. Fill six fields. Most of the value is in fields two and six.

Copy this file into any assistant as a custom instruction, or keep it open and
fill it by hand. Nothing to install.

---

## The card

```
SOURCE:        what you are checking, in one line

CLAIM:         restate it in the source's own words.
               Do not upgrade it into your vocabulary.

OPEN TERMS:    words doing real work here with no criteria given.
               Write "none" only if you checked.

PARALLEL:      if an analogy is carrying weight, name the relation it copies.
               "A cannot become B, same as X cannot become Y."
               Write "none" if there is no analogy. None is common.

HINGE:         the one fact that would change this. If you cannot name one,
               say so. That is a finding about the claim.

STATUS:        accept  |  not yet  |  I may be wrong about ___
```

---

## Six rules that make it work

**Restating is not agreeing.** The first field is a translation test. If you
cannot restate the claim in its own terms, you do not yet know what it says, and
anything you write after that is about something else.

**"None" and "not yet" are complete answers.** They are not failures to fill the
form. A paragraph with no analogy and no hinge is a low signal paragraph, and
finding that out in one minute is the point.

**Never invent a term to fill a field.** If nothing is open, nothing is open.
Padding the card teaches you to trust it when it is empty.

**"Not yet" does not become "accept" by rereading.** It changes when a named
thing changes: a source arrives, a term gets criteria, the hinge resolves. Write
what changed next to the new status.

**One card per thing.** Do not run it on a whole paper. Run it on the paragraph
that is about to change what you do.

**Pick by rule, not by interest.** Card the first thing today that would change
what you do next, whatever it happens to be. If you choose the interesting one,
you will keep choosing claims where the card performs well and never run it
where it could catch you. That produces a week of accepts and a false
conclusion that the card is a rubber stamp.

---

## The fourteen card test

Two questions, and the first one settles it on its own.

**Primary, ask it weekly:** did this change at least one thing you were about to
accept? One clear yes is enough to keep the card. "It feels rigorous" does not
count. If the answer is no after two weeks, stop, or change the trigger. Never
add fields.

**Secondary, the tally.** Keep it for fourteen cards, not seven:

```
accept                   |||| |||| ||
not yet                  ||
I may be wrong about ___ |
OPEN TERMS = none        |||| ||
PARALLEL = none          |||| |||| |
```

Fourteen rather than seven because seven cannot tell "never fires" from "fires
rarely." If the true rate of `not yet` is one in five, seven cards come back
empty 21 percent of the time and you would retire a working card on noise. At
fourteen that drops to 4 percent. Seven extra minutes buys the difference.

Reading the tally:

- **Always `accept`, and the primary question is also no.** The card is a rubber
  stamp. Check the pick rule first: you were probably choosing interesting
  claims rather than the next one in front of you.
- **`I may be wrong about ___` never fires.** Expected. It is the socially
  expensive option. Record the zero as data rather than treating the field as
  broken.
- **`OPEN TERMS` always empty.** You are reading past the vocabulary. Try it on
  a paragraph of your own writing.
- **Rejecting outcomes fire but nothing you do changes.** The card is working as
  an observation and failing as a brake. That is a trigger problem, not a card
  problem.

Adding fields is the failure mode. If the card is not working, the answer is a
different trigger or no card, never a longer card.

## Worked example

```
SOURCE:      a project summary quoting an upper bound of 0.8420473

CLAIM:       the eight cell bank is still open, and computing the full
             matrix is the decisive next step

OPEN TERMS:  "decisive". Decisive for what, under which threshold?

PARALLEL:    none

HINGE:       whether a later calculation already tightened that bound.
             If one did, the summary is describing a superseded state.

STATUS:      not yet
```

That took under a minute and was correct. A later calculation had tightened the
bound and closed the branch. The summary was quoting a real number from a
superseded step, which no amount of rereading the summary would have revealed.
