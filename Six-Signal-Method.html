One light cannot tell the whole truth.

The Six-Signal Method keeps different questions separate. It makes missing evidence visible, limits what each answer can mean, and prevents a convenient color from becoming a claim the records do not support.

Educational method only. This page reads no inputs and reports no status about a system, site, AI, person, or deployment.

Six questions, held apart

A real implementation needs typed evidence and a named policy for every answer. If that basis is absent, the honest result is UNKNOWN—not a guessed pass, failure, or confidence score.

  1. Calibration

    Can establish
    Whether every required check, observation, ripple check, and response check passed under one named policy.
    UNKNOWN means
    At least one required input is missing, stale, incompatible, or undecided.
    Cannot establish
    Universal safety, correctness outside the declared scope, or future behavior.

  2. Consequence

    Can establish
    Whether a named, bounded consequence is supported and whether the next consequential step requires an explicit hold.
    UNKNOWN means
    No qualified consequence observation and policy binding are available.
    Cannot establish
    Permission, acknowledgement, or authority to perform the next action.

  3. Evidence

    Can establish
    Whether the declared evidence set is present, attributable, and sufficient for the named check.
    UNKNOWN means
    Coverage, provenance, or an applicable decision rule is incomplete.
    Cannot establish
    That a claim is true merely because many records or citations exist.

  4. Integrity

    Can establish
    Whether selected bytes, hashes, sequence links, and deterministic replay agree with their declared records.
    UNKNOWN means
    The required binding, replay, or independent check was not completed.
    Cannot establish
    Semantic truth, authorship, safety, or correctness of intact content.

  5. Privacy

    Can establish
    Whether specified storage, disclosure, and egress checks passed inside an explicit boundary.
    UNKNOWN means
    The applicable data flow or control has not been completely observed and verified.
    Cannot establish
    General privacy, legal compliance, or safety beyond the inspected scope.

  6. Activity

    Can establish
    Whether a named, bounded process is observed as pending, running, complete, or otherwise explicitly recorded.
    UNKNOWN means
    No qualified activity observation exists; silence is not evidence of idleness.
    Cannot establish
    System health, user intent, or successful outcome by itself.

UNKNOWN is a result. It preserves the difference between “we measured a null,” “this does not apply,” and “we did not observe it.” Those states must not collapse into the same empty field.

A small reproducible example

The companion Pinned Snapshot Card names seven already-public files at one public Git commit and records their Git object IDs, SHA-256 digests, and byte lengths. Its verifier checks identity only. It deliberately publishes no private evaluation, lamp state, or live result.

Inspect the machine-readable card.

This method is a candidate research interface, not a standard or certification. Each implementation must publish its own policy, evidence boundary, rejection tests, and claim ceiling.
